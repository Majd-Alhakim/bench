A glass box with a team inside.

Other agent teams feel like magic and hide the bill. This one feels like a well-lit studio: you see who is working, on what, and what it costs, and nothing leaves without you.

Essence: in plain sight.

What it is
Your own company of agents on your desktop, with every step, prompt, token and file open to you.
How it should feel
Calm control. Like looking into a workshop through clean glass: busy, warm, nothing hidden.
What it promises
You see it before it happens, you can take it back, and the team gets better at working for you.
Candidnot clinical

Says what it did, what it costs and what it couldn’t do, in plain words. Numbers over adjectives.

Calmnot sleepy

Quiet chrome, alive characters. Nothing flashes, nothing pings without a reason, and the team still feels present.

Warmnot cute

Characters with eyes, never mascots with jokes. No exclamation marks, no emoji in the interface.

Precisenot technical

Exact times, counts and percentages. Jargon stays in the trace, one click away.

Deferentialnot passive

You are the founder. The team proposes, drafts and asks; it never acts outward on its own.

The signature is the Look.

Two slanted strokes. They are every agent’s face, the way you open the glass box, and the way the team asks for you. One shape, three jobs, used nowhere else.

The stroke is a pill rotated 12°, about 2.6 times as tall as it is wide. The eyes stay on the faces; the logo is the team’s shapes (Quartet, below).

A face for every agent

Hue and shape change; the eyes never do. That family resemblance is what makes ninety agents feel like one team.

Look inside, anywhere

The glass box has one handle. The eyes glyph opens the trace of any message, cost, file or memory note: same glyph, same place, everywhere.

Quartet: the team is the logo.

Four seats, four different characters: a sphere, a triangle, a rounded square and a droplet, cut from the same bodies your agents wear. Every agent you create is one of these shapes, so the mark means “your team” and survives any name.

Tinted area: clear space, a third of the mark’s height on every side. Nothing enters it.

On Porcelain and white: Ink, one colour.

On Ink: the same shapes in Porcelain white. Never boxed or inverted.

Smallest: 16 px. Below 24 px the mark stands alone, without the name.

In colour only on the app icon and brand moments. Coral, Honey, Mint, Orchid, fixed: they never follow your agents.

Construction

  • A 100-unit square split in four; each character sits in its own 50-unit seat
  • Sphere r 20 · triangle with 6-unit round joins · square 38 at r 12 · droplet at 0.46
  • Gaps of 8 units or more, so the four still separate at 16 px
  • Lockup: mark, gap 0.36 × its height, the name in Instrument Sans 600 at 0.84 × height, −0.035em
  • Never four squares (that’s a tile logo), never a fifth shape, never rearranged
  • Never one shape in an agent’s hue, never outlined, never on Cobalt

Rename grammar: any 3 to 8 lowercase letters take the same slot. The name is “eucas”.

Four seats on dark glass.

Ink glass, a soft light along the top edge, and the four characters in full colour. It’s the one place the mark wears colour, so it reads as a bright, friendly object in a dock or taskbar and stays legible at 16 px.

You, from the main agent.

The app speaks to you

As the main agent would: “What needs you”, “You’re all caught up”. Never “me” or “my” for you.

Agents write in lowercase

Short, concrete, first person: “on it.”, “done.” The interface stays in sentence case.

Numbers, then words

The exact time, count and cost first; reassurance second; one next step last.

Three sounds, one family.

Soft, short and pitched in one key, never an alarm. Each one tells you something, so they’re on by default. Mute them in Settings › Notifications; Focus and Do Not Disturb silence them all.

Nothing plays until you press a button.

Two other directions we weighed.

Both are coherent. Neither does both halves of the job, the glass box and the team, as well as the Look.

B · The Thread

An instrument. The signature is one live line that runs through every step, changes hue at each handoff and opens the trace anywhere you touch it. Ink on white, numbers forward, almost no illustration.

Why not first: precise but cold. It serves the glass box and loses the team; the characters you asked for become dots on a line, and thin lines everywhere drift into ornament.

C · Daylight Studio

Warm and plush. Big dimensional characters everywhere, each screen washed in the colour of whoever you’re with, rounder type, a playful greeting on every first open.

Why not first: lovely in a demo, tiring at hour six. Cute wins over premium, and serious moments (a cost, a denied approval, a delete) lose their weight next to plush faces.
