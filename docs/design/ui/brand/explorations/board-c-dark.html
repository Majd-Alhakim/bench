01 · The idea

A glasshouse at night

A glasshouse is the most honest building there is: at night, with the lights on, you see everything inside from anywhere. That is Bench's promise, made into a place. The team are lights inside it. You hold the lamp.

The essence

Lit from within. The interface doesn't light the work from outside with spotlights and effects; the work carries its own light. Agents glow when they work, dim when they pause, and look up when they need you.

Why it fits Bench

  • Glass box → glasshouse. The product's core promise becomes the brand's place.
  • A team you can see. Lights in a dark room read at a glance: who is busy, who is waiting.
  • Calm for all-day use. Dark, low-glare, warm; built for evenings and long sessions.
  • Survives a rename. The mark is an arch window, not a word.

What it refuses

  • No blue anywhere that means action or identity (Grok Bot's colour, and everyone else's).
  • No stark black and white monospace. The ground is green-black loam, the text is mist, the type is soft.
  • No cream and clay. Nothing that reads as Anthropic or Claude.
  • No frosted blur. Glass here means clear panes and hairline frames, never haze.
UnhurriedNot sleepy. Light warms up and settles; nothing flashes.
CandidNot clinical. Numbers first, in plain words.
WarmNot cute. Eyes, light and a soft serif; no mascots, no jokes.
PreciseNot technical. Exact times and costs; jargon stays inside.
DeferentialNot passive. The team drafts and asks; you decide.
02 · Mark and app icon

The lit window

03 · Characters

The team as lanterns

The same family: non-human shapes with eyes, nothing else. In board C they are rendered as lanterns: a body of coloured glass lit from inside, with a soft halo, and eyes that are the dark places where the light isn't. Light carries state: working is bright and steady, thinking breathes, paused is turned low.

05 · Type

A soft serif and a clear sans

Fraunces (OFL) with its Soft axis at 100: round, warm, a little old-world, like lettering painted on a glasshouse door. Used only for the wordmark and the one headline per screen. Figtree (OFL) does all the work: friendly, open, very legible on dark at 12–15 px. Both are bundled with the app, so every OS looks the same.

07 · Motion

Light, not leaps

Board C moves the way lamps do: things warm up and settle, they don't bounce or fly in. Chrome slides on critically damped springs; only light and eyes move on their own. Demos loop here; in the app each plays once, on the event. With reduced motion the end state arrives at once and the words carry the change.

08 · Voice

The metaphor stays in the pixels

The glasshouse is how Bench looks, never how it talks. No "lights on", no glow puns. The app speaks to you as the main agent would, in the second person; agents write in lowercase, short and concrete. After-hours calm: numbers first, reassurance second, one next step last.

09 · Sound

Glass and felt, in a quiet room

Three sounds, one family: struck glass and a felt-mallet wooden bar, played softly with a short room tail. Pitched in one key so they never clash. On by default (your decision), one mute switch in Settings › Sound, and Focus or Do Not Disturb silences everything. Never a sound for agents talking to each other, for typing, or for anything you didn't need to hear.
