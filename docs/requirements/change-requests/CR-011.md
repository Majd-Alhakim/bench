## Change

**1. One Euca, created at setup: an agentsitter.**
- **At setup the user creates their one Euca:** a name and a look (a non-human shape avatar in the brand style, BRAND §avatars), its rules (the tested library plus their own; D-4 as in CR-010) and its settings.
- **The Euca never writes code or does project work.** It watches over the user's Claude Code sessions: it guards (limits, tripwire, heartbeat), tells you what needs you, shows usage, and supervises (Pro, and in the trial and taste).

**2. It lives in the notch** (reusing the notch design from CR-009 and the 2026-10-25 design, adapted to the companion):
- **The Euca's eyes** sit in the notch.
- **The glow** shows that sessions are working.
- **The eyes show the guard's state:** awake means protected; a clear "not protected" state when the heartbeat fails.
- **Something needs you:** a small animation and a marker that stays. **It never opens by itself;** you hover to see it.
- **A click opens the panel.**

**3. The panel has three parts and a list. There's no big main window.**
- **Needs you:** a short list at the top of the panel, *as well as* the notch markers (client: "both").
- **Settings:**
  - the Euca's name and look;
  - protection and the admin lock;
  - privacy (Level 1/2);
  - sounds;
  - the diagnostic;
  - Pro and the trial.
- **Rules:**
  - the library on/off;
  - your own rules;
  - prompted rules (described in plain words, drafted as exact rules, confirmed);
  - which rules are locked.
- **Usage:**
  - where your Claude usage went;
  - when you'll hit your limit (forecast);
  - **the supervisor meter.**

The 7-day retro becomes part of setup and stays reachable from Usage. Same-file warnings and tripwire stops arrive as needs-you items. The tray icon remains only as the Windows fallback (quit, show the panel), not as the main surface.

**4. A visible supervisor meter, never in dollars.**
- The meter shows how much supervisor help is left this month, as a bar or percentage, plus the reset date.
- **Internally the limit is a hidden model-cost budget per user, not a call count.** Each prompt is capped at about 2,000 tokens.
- When the meter is empty, the Euca goes back to asking you, saying so in plain words: *"Your Euca will check with you more often until 1 Nov."*

**5. Free users get a taste of the supervisor.**
- **A 14-day full trial at install:** the full supervisor, with a hidden cost cap of about $0.50 per trial (about $0.11 for an average user).
- **Then a small monthly taste on Free:** a hidden cap of about $0.02 a month, roughly 80 supervised decisions, shown on the same meter.
- **One trial per verified email,** which also builds the mailing list. The supervisor still needs Level 2 consent to read the agent's last message (CR-010 data rules).
- Pro stays $20/month, with the first 200 payers at $15 for life.

**6. Design approval gate.**
- The companion UI in PR #57 and the landing page in PR #64 were **never approved as designs** by the client; #57 was merged without a design review.
- **The UX and UI are redone for the notch-and-panel model** and need the client's **explicit design approval before anything downstream uses them** (landing page, build).
- The landing page (#64) waits for the approved design.

## Reason

The client wants a simpler product with one clear character: a single Euca that sits in the notch and minds your agents, with three plain sections. That is calmer and more distinctive than a multi-section main window. A free taste of the supervisor lets people feel its value before paying. Showing the meter without dollars keeps trust without exposing internal costs.

## Impact analysis

- **Cost (trial and taste, Jev + gpt-oss prices):**
  - At about 1,000 new installs a month, the trials cost **about $110/month**.
  - The taste costs at most about $0.02 per free user a month.
  - **Worst case with 10,000 free users and nobody paying: about $300/month.**
  - With 10,000 users and 2% paying, the profit of about $2,600/month absorbs $100–300.
- **Effort:** the redesign is about 1–2 days of design work. The build is a similar size; the notch replaces the main window rather than adding to it. The notch window is a new risk for the shell spike (SP-ST1 should test it).
- **Risk:**
  - **Trial abuse:** one verified email, plus device binding.
  - **The notch** on Windows (always-on-top, focus, click-through, full-screen apps) needs to be in the spikes.
